# Project Limitations

## Public Data Only

This project relies only on publicly available information, such as company websites, reports, press releases, and other accessible sources. Because internal systems, policies, incidents, investments, and controls are not visible, the scores may not reflect a company’s full technology readiness or risk posture. A strong or weak score should therefore be interpreted as an assessment of visible public evidence, not as a complete audit of the organization.

## Evidence Gaps

Some companies publish detailed information about AI, privacy, cybersecurity, and governance, while others disclose very little. This creates uneven evidence across the dataset and can make companies with stronger public communication appear more mature than companies with similar internal capabilities but less public documentation. Missing evidence is not automatically proof that a company lacks a capability.

## Scoring Subjectivity

The scoring framework uses defined categories and a consistent rubric, but some judgment is still required when translating research notes into 1–5 scores. Different reviewers may interpret the same evidence differently, especially when public information is incomplete or unclear. Manual quality checks reduce inconsistency, but they do not remove subjectivity completely.

## AI Output Limitations

AI-generated summaries and recommendations are based only on the structured notes and source material provided in the dataset. They are not based on exhaustive independent research and may leave out important context that was not included in the notes. All AI outputs require human review for accuracy, relevance, unsupported claims, and consulting quality before they are approved for use.

## Version 1 Scope Constraints

Version 1 does not include a deployed web application, user accounts, private company data, paid data sources, or machine learning models. It uses a structured dataset, scoring framework, AI-assisted analysis, and Power BI dashboard to demonstrate the project concept. These scope limits keep the project realistic, but they also reduce automation, real-time updates, and depth of analysis.

## Company Coverage

The project covers approximately 40–80 companies, depending on the final dataset size. This provides a useful sample for comparison, but it is not a comprehensive view of the full market or every industry. Findings should be treated as patterns within the selected sample rather than conclusions about all companies.
